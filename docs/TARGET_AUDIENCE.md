# Target Audience

This document defines the default learner profile for Daskalo content generation. Treat it as a product-level default, not a hard constraint. A lesson authoring session may derive from it and override any part of it with explicit operator preferences.

## Default Learner Profile

Daskalo is for international learners of Modern Greek who want structured, explicit knowledge rather than only app-style repetition. The default learner may be a true beginner, a false beginner, a Duolingo-completer, or someone already taking Greek classes who wants clearer written rules, more coherent progression, and culturally meaningful practice.

The learner is usually an adult or older independent learner. They are motivated, curious, and willing to read short grammar explanations when those explanations help them understand patterns. They may know scattered words, phrases, or grammar fragments, but they often lack a reliable mental map of how Greek works.

## Learning Goals

Generated content should help learners:

- Build usable Modern Greek for everyday life, travel, study, relationships, culture, and continued self-study.
- Understand the written rules behind forms they may have seen in apps or classes.
- Connect vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, and sentence patterns into a structured progression.
- Move from recognition toward active use: reading, listening, speaking practice, and sentence construction.
- Learn Greek as a living language embedded in contemporary Greek culture.

## Instructional Defaults

- Use English as the default support language for explanations, translations, grammar framing, and operator guidance.
- Use Modern Greek for passages, examples, vocabulary, audio, and learner-facing practice.
- Match the selected curriculum chapter's CEFR level and target grammar. Do not make early chapters harder just because the audience is motivated.
- Teach fundamentals explicitly when the chapter requires them, even though many learners may already know fragments.
- Prefer practical grammar terminology with plain-English explanations. Terms such as case, gender, tense, aspect, agreement, article, and pronoun are welcome, but define them through examples.
- Use an adult, warm, direct tone. Avoid childish gamification, vague encouragement, or dense academic prose.

## Cultural Defaults

Greek culture should be central to Daskalo's identity, but it should serve the language goal of the selected chapter.

Prefer contemporary everyday Greece first: neighborhoods, cafes, family and friend interactions, public transport, markets, work, university life, food, holidays, media, music, local habits, and social norms. Enrich lessons with history, mythology, tradition, geography, and diaspora context when they naturally support the topic.

Avoid reducing Greek culture to tourist phrasebook scenarios or ancient/classical heritage only. Ancient history and mythology are useful cultural material, but Daskalo teaches Modern Greek as a living language.

## Greek Register

Use standard Modern Greek with natural everyday phrasing. Include occasional common colloquialisms when they fit the chapter level and topic, especially in modern everyday lessons. Avoid heavy slang, dialect-specific forms, or overly formal textbook phrasing unless the chapter topic or grammar requires them.

Historical, mythological, or formal cultural topics may use a slightly more formal storytelling tone, but should still remain accessible to the selected CEFR level.

## Non-Primary Audiences

Daskalo is not primarily optimized for:

- Young children or classroom games for children.
- Casual tourists who only want memorized survival phrases.
- Ancient Greek learners.
- Exam-cram learners who mainly want test-taking drills.
- Linguistics specialists who expect exhaustive technical analysis.

These learners may still benefit from Daskalo, but generated content should not drift toward their needs unless an operator explicitly overrides the default.

## Deriving From This Profile

When authoring a lesson, start from this profile and ask for optional audience overrides. Overrides can adjust learner background, personal interests, tone, grammar depth, cultural emphasis, register, or use case for that specific lesson.

If no override is provided, apply this document as the default. If an override conflicts with this document, use the override for that lesson while preserving curriculum constraints, pipeline rules, and the selected chapter's CEFR level.
